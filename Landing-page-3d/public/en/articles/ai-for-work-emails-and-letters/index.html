AI for Everyone

AI for Work Emails and Letters: Fast but Still Personal

Email is the task AI helps with most: drafting, polishing tone, changing length. The problem is that raw output often sounds generic. The key is to give context and adjust before sending.

By Muhammad Muchson Attoyibi·October 23, 2026·1 min read

Key takeaways

  • A strong request names the recipient, the points to include, the tone and the length, and asks for a clear subject line.
  • Raw AI output reads generic; add specific names and details and swap stock phrases for your own style.
  • Give an old email of yours as a style sample and ask the AI to imitate it.
  • Check names, dates, figures and promises before sending, and never send sensitive emails without reading every sentence.

Before sending

To sound like you

  • Give an old email of yours as an example and ask it to imitate the style.
  • Delete over-flowery opening sentences.
  • Use specific names and details, not general sentences.
  • Read it aloud. If it sounds odd, change it.
Be careful

Do not send sensitive emails (financial, legal, personal) without checking every sentence. AI can add promises or facts you did not mean.

FAQ

Will the recipient know the email was made by AI?
It can show if it is too generic. Personalising and editing make it natural. If asked, be honest about using a tool.
Is it safe to put email content into AI?
It depends on the service's policy and the content. Avoid sensitive data; disguise names and numbers if needed.
How do I reply to a long email?
Paste the email (without sensitive data), then ask for a summary of the points that need an answer and a draft reply for each point.
