Eisenhower Matrix: Decide What to Do First

A long to-do list makes us do what is loudest, not what is most valuable. The Eisenhower Matrix helps separate what is urgent from what is important, two things that often get mixed up.

By Muhammad Muchson Attoyibi·October 23, 2026·1 min read

Key takeaways

  • Sort each task with two questions: is it important, and is it urgent?
  • Do important and urgent tasks first, schedule important but not urgent ones, delegate or limit urgent but unimportant ones, and drop the rest.
  • The important-but-not-urgent quadrant is where long-term results are made, and it is the easiest to forget.
  • Constantly putting out fires usually means important tasks were never scheduled.

Why it matters

If you keep putting out "fires", many important tasks may never have been scheduled and so turned urgent. Giving time to what is important prevents crises.

FAQ

Who invented this matrix?
It is attributed to Dwight D. Eisenhower, who was known for distinguishing urgent from important. The matrix form was popularised later by management authors.
What if everything feels important?
Ask: what happens if this is not done, and to whom? That helps sort out the real priorities.
What if my manager marks everything as urgent?
Discuss priorities openly. Show the consequences of doing everything at once.
