Your First Freelance Job: Finding Clients and Setting Prices

Being a freelancer is not just technical ability. You also sell, negotiate and manage projects. The good news is that the first client usually comes from your closest circle, and the basics can be learned.

By Muhammad Muchson Attoyibi·October 23, 2026·1 min read

Key takeaways

  • Start with a specific service whose result is clear, such as "fast landing pages for small businesses".
  • Your first client usually comes from people who already know you: friends, communities, local businesses and alumni.
  • Work out your rate from your income target and the share of hours you can really bill, then add a reserve (for example 20%) for revisions and risk.
  • Never start without a written agreement: scope, deliverables, schedule, price and payment terms, number of revisions and how to stop or change the project.

Start with a specific service

"I can build anything" is hard to sell. "I build fast landing pages for small businesses" is easy to understand. A specific service makes it easier for clients to see that you answer their need, and makes it easier for you to improve your process.

FAQ

Where do I find my first client?
Start with your close network: friends, communities, local businesses and alumni. Then freelance platforms and professional media.
Do I need a freelance platform?
It is useful for starting and building reputation, but there are usually fees and competition. Combine it with your direct network.
How do I deal with a client who keeps asking for revisions?
Go back to the agreement: the number of revisions included, and politely offer an extra fee beyond that.
