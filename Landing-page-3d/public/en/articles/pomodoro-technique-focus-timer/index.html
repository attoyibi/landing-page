The Pomodoro Technique: Focus for 25 Minutes (Live Timer)

Hard to start a big task, then easily distracted? The Pomodoro technique lowers the starting load with one small promise: full focus for only 25 minutes, then a break. Simple, but effective for many people.

By Muhammad Muchson Attoyibi·October 23, 2026·1 min read

Key takeaways

  • Work 25 minutes, rest 5, repeat four times, then take a longer break of 15-30 minutes.
  • A clear time limit makes starting feel light, and regular breaks reduce fatigue and keep focus quality up.
  • Write down distractions and put them off until the break instead of answering at once.
  • Adjust it to yourself (25/5, 50/10 or 15/3), and do not force a break if you are deeply absorbed.

The rules

Why it can help

  • A clear time limit makes starting feel light.
  • Regular breaks reduce fatigue and keep focus quality.
  • Counting pomodoros gives a real picture of how long a task takes.
  • Distractions are noted and put off to the break, not answered at once.

Adapting it to you

Flexible

If you are deeply absorbed in a task, there is no need to break off by force. The technique is a tool, not a rule. Use it mainly when it is hard to start or you are easily distracted.

FAQ

What if I am interrupted mid-session?
Note the short interruption and return. If it is important, end the session and start again later.
Does it suit studying?
Yes, very much. Combine it with recall practice at the end of each session.
How many pomodoros a day is reasonable?
Many people reach about 6 to 10 quality focus sessions a day. Start with what is realistic.
