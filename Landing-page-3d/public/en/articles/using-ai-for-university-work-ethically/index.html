AI for Everyone

Using AI for University Work Ethically

Many students ask whether using AI for assignments is cheating. The answer depends on your lecturer's and university's rules, and on how you use it. The general principle is simple: AI may help you learn, but not replace your learning.

By Muhammad Muchson Attoyibi·October 23, 2026·1 min read

Key takeaways

  • Reasonable uses include explaining hard concepts, creating practice questions, grammar feedback and explaining code and errors.
  • Problematic uses include submitting AI output as your own, inventing citations, doing exams with AI and using it when it is banned.
  • Check every citation and reference against the original source, because AI can invent them.
  • Know the course policy, be transparent about AI help, and ask your lecturer clearly if unsure.

Habits that protect you

About AI detectors

AI-writing detectors are not fully reliable and can accuse wrongly. Do not depend on them, whether you are a student or a lecturer. The safest thing is to be transparent and able to explain your own work.

How to ask your lecturer

Say it clearly: "May I use AI to check grammar and explain concepts, but not to write the content of the assignment? How should I acknowledge it?" A question like this shows good intent and avoids misunderstandings.

FAQ

Is using AI automatically plagiarism?
Not automatically. Problems arise when the output is handed in as your own work without permission or disclosure, or contains fake citations.
Do I need to disclose AI use?
If the course policy requires it, yes. Honestly stating AI help is usually safer.
How do I keep learning effective with AI?
Try it yourself first, then ask the AI to check or explain. Re-explaining in your own words makes learning stick.
