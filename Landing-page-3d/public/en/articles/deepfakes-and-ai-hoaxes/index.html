Deepfakes and AI Hoaxes: 8 Ways to Spot Fake Content

Tools that make images, voices and videos are easier to reach, and they are also used for scams and spreading hoaxes. There is no single way to spot them, but the habit of checking before believing can stop most of the spread.

By Muhammad Muchson Attoyibi·October 23, 2026·1 min read

Key takeaways

  • Check the source, the context, odd details, movement and voice, and whether the content tries to make you panic or angry.
  • Search the image in reverse and look for confirmation from trusted media.
  • For urgent requests for money, confirm with the person through another channel before acting.
  • A 30-second pause before sharing stops many hoaxes.

Eight things you can check

  1. Source. Who first uploaded it? An official account or an anonymous one?
  2. Context. Is there coverage from trusted media about the same event?
  3. Odd details. Fingers, teeth, ears, shadows and background text are often strange in AI images.
  4. Movement and sound. In video, blinking, lip sync and intonation can feel unnatural.
  5. Excessive emotion. Content that makes you angry or panicked is often designed to spread fast.
  6. Reverse image search. Use reverse image search to see where an image came from.
  7. Urgent requests. Fake-voice scammers often ask for a transfer or a code in a hurry.
  8. Ask directly. Contact the person through another channel you trust.

FAQ

Can deepfake detection tools be relied on?
They help, but they are not perfect and are in a constant race with the technology that makes fakes. Combine them with source verification.
Are all AI images hoaxes?
No. Many are made for art and entertainment. A hoax is when an image is used to deceive or mislead.
What should I do if my face is used without permission?
Keep evidence, report it to the platform, and if needed to the authorities. Use the official reporting services.
