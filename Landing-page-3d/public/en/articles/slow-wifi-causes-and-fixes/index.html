Slow Wi-Fi? Common Causes and How to Fix Them

Before changing your internet plan, it is worth checking the simple things. Many cases of slow Wi-Fi are caused by where the router sits, signal interference or devices hogging speed, not the plan you pay for.

By Muhammad Muchson Attoyibi·October 23, 2026·1 min read

Key takeaways

  • First find where the problem is: slow on every device points to the modem, router or provider; slow on one device points to that device or its distance.
  • Put the router in the middle of the home, high up and in the open, away from microwaves, speakers and thick walls.
  • Use 5 GHz for nearby devices and 2.4 GHz for longer range, and use a LAN cable for devices that do not move.
  • Test speed with a cable to the router to tell a Wi-Fi problem from a plan problem; the speed on the plan is a maximum.

Keep in mind

The speed shown on the plan is the maximum speed from the provider to your home. The speed on your device can be lower because of Wi-Fi, distance and network load.

FAQ

What is the difference between 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz?
2.4 GHz reaches further but is slower and more crowded with interference. 5 GHz is faster but has a shorter range.
Does a signal booster (repeater) help?
It can extend the range, but sometimes lowers the speed. A mesh system is usually better for a large home.
When should I replace the router?
When it is very old, does not support newer Wi-Fi standards, or often cuts out and is unstable after a reset.
