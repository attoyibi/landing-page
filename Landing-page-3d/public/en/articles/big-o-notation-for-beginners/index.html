Big O Notation for Beginners: Measuring Algorithm Speed

Two programs that are both correct can differ hugely in speed once the data grows. Big O is the language for talking about that difference without having to time them with a stopwatch on one particular computer.

By Muhammad Muchson Attoyibi·October 23, 2026·1 min read

Key takeaways

  • Big O measures how the number of steps grows with the amount of data (n), not real seconds.
  • Common classes: O(1) constant, O(log n), O(n) linear, O(n log n), O(n²) quadratic, O(2ⁿ) exponential.
  • One loop is O(n), a loop inside a loop is O(n²), halving at each step is O(log n).
  • Constants and small terms are ignored (3n + 10 is O(n)); Big O matters when data is large or the work is repeated.

What is measured: growth, not seconds

Big O does not say how many seconds, but how the number of steps grows as the amount of data (n) gets bigger. If the data doubles, does the work stay the same, double, or become four times as much? That is what it answers.

How to read code

A simplification

Big O simplifies: constants and small terms are ignored. 3n + 10 is still called O(n). That is because when n is very large, the dominant term decides.

When does Big O matter?

With small data, almost every algorithm feels fast. Big O becomes important when data grows: tens of thousands of users, millions of rows, or calculations that run repeatedly. Then choosing O(n log n) over O(n²) can mean the difference between seconds and hours.

FAQ

Does Big O measure real time?
No. It measures how the steps grow relative to the data size. Real time also depends on hardware and implementation.
What is the difference between best, average and worst case?
The best case is the most favourable input, the worst is the least favourable, and the average is the general picture. Usually the worst or average case is discussed.
Should I always pick the smallest Big O?
Not always. Code simplicity, data size and memory also matter.
