Active Recall: Why Testing Yourself Beats Re-Reading

Many students study by re-reading and highlighting their notes. It feels familiar, so it feels like understanding. That familiarity is misleading. Education research consistently finds that testing yourself is far more effective than re-reading.

By Muhammad Muchson Attoyibi·October 23, 2026·1 min read

Key takeaways

  • Re-reading feels familiar but gives little; testing yourself strengthens memory.
  • Close your notes and write what you remember, do practice questions without looking at the answers, and explain ideas in your own words.
  • Struggling to remember is a sign you are learning, not a sign of failure.
  • Use AI to write the questions, not to give you the answers.

FAQ

Why does it feel harder?
Because your brain is working to retrieve the information. That productive difficulty is exactly what strengthens memory.
Is re-reading completely useless?
It is useful for first exposure, but the results are far smaller than testing yourself.
How long should a self-testing session be?
Short but frequent. For example 10-20 minutes after studying, and again a few days later.
