AI for Everyone

How to Write a Good Prompt: The 5-Part Formula

The difference between an ordinary AI answer and a very useful one often lies in how you ask. The good news is that there is a simple formula anyone can use, with no technical terms.

By Muhammad Muchson Attoyibi·October 23, 2026·1 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good prompt has up to five parts: role, context, task, format and constraints.
  • Context is what stops the AI guessing; the format decides whether you get a table, points or an email.
  • Ask for examples, work step by step, give a style sample, let the AI ask you questions and ask it to check itself.
  • A good prompt does not guarantee a correct answer, so always check facts, numbers, quotes and names.

Tips that often make the difference

  1. Ask for examples. "Give two examples with a short reason."
  2. Ask step by step. For a big task, start with an outline and then go section by section.
  3. Give a style sample. Paste a paragraph you like as a reference.
  4. Ask the AI to question you. "Ask me about anything unclear before answering."
  5. Ask it to check itself. "Review your answer: are there any uncertain claims?"
Remember

A good prompt does not guarantee a correct answer. Always check facts, numbers, quotes and names. Treat the AI as a fast assistant that still needs supervision.

FAQ

Is a longer prompt always better?
No. What matters is that it is clear and relevant. A long, messy prompt can confuse.
Do I need to use English?
Not necessarily. Modern models understand many languages well. Use the language that is clearest for you.
What is prompt engineering?
The craft of writing instructions so the AI gives the best result. At heart it is clear communication.
