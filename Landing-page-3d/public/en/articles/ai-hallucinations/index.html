AI Hallucinations: Why AI Makes Things Up and What to Do

One of the biggest risks of using a chatbot is an answer that is convincing but wrong: a fictional quote, an invented figure, or the name of a person who does not exist. This is not a strange bug, but a result of how the model works. Understanding it makes you more careful.

By Muhammad Muchson Attoyibi·October 23, 2026·2 min read

Key takeaways

  • Language models are trained to produce fluent text, not to check the truth, so they can state false things confidently.
  • Typical hallucinations: invented quotes, dead links, made-up statistics and code that calls functions that do not exist.
  • Ask for sources, open them yourself, and compare answers from different wordings.
  • You can lower the risk by asking the AI to answer only from documents you supply, but still check the result.

Why does AI make things up?

A language model is trained to guess the most plausible next word based on patterns from a huge amount of text. Its goal is to produce text that is fluent and natural, not to check the truth. When it does not know, it is still driven to compose a sentence that sounds right. The result can be a false fact wrapped in a convincing style. This phenomenon is called a hallucination.

Common examples of hallucinations

  • Quotes from books, journals or court rulings that never existed.
  • Links that do not open.
  • Statistics that look specific but have no basis.
  • Biographies that mix up facts about different people.
  • Code that calls functions or libraries that do not exist.

How to check an AI answer

Reducing the risk

A way to lower the risk: ask the AI to answer only from documents you provide and to say "I do not know" if the answer is not there. This technique is used in document-based systems (RAG). Still check the result.

FAQ

Do all AIs hallucinate?
Almost all language models can. The rate varies, and developers keep reducing the problem, but it has not gone away.
Does a search feature make AI always right?
It helps, because answers refer to sources, but the AI can still misread or misreport a source. Open the source.
Is a hallucination the same as lying?
No. The model has no intention to deceive. It produces text that looks plausible without knowing whether it is true.
