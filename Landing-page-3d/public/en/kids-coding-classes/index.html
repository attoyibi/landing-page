Why learn with me

A mentor who teaches and designs curricula

I mentor children learning to code at Algonova, and for over five years I have designed curricula and trained learners across many programmes. This class follows the ADDIE framework and Bloom's taxonomy: each lesson has a measurable goal, not just a video to watch.

Measured results in the programmes I run

In the national training programmes I managed, 400+ learners and 20+ mentors reached a 97% graduation rate with 4.9 out of 5 satisfaction, and I was named Best Mentor for the React Front-End programme. I use the same approach with children: track progress, give quick feedback, celebrate results.

Learning to think, not only to play

Children practise step-by-step thinking, breaking problems down and carefully hunting for mistakes (debugging), with graduated hints when they get stuck.

What children learn

  • Module 1 · Introduction. Algorithms, loops, starting position and events
  • Module 2 · Space. Coordinates, direction, degrees, the pen and messages
  • Module 3 · Games. Conditions, procedures and making a game
  • Module 4 · Logic. and/or/not, conditional loops, random numbers, a team project
  • Module 5 · Variables. Score, lives, time, parameters and a chatbot
  • Module 6 · Clones. Classes, objects, local and global variables, a final project
  • Module 7 · Graduation. A recap, a final exam and a showcase of their work

Every lesson, 4 fun steps

  1. A short illustrated explanation (3–5 pages) with block examples they can see straight away.
  2. Robot practice: graduated puzzles on sequence, loops, conditions and variables.
  3. A project in the Studio: making animations and games with Scratch-style blocks.
  4. A quick quiz to review, then a short story shared with the teacher.

For parents

  • Clear progress: finished lessons, stars, projects and a graduation certificate.
  • Flexible timing. Suggested: twice a week, 45–60 minutes.
  • A skill that is not locked in: projects can be downloaded and opened in Scratch.

Frequently asked questions

What age are these kids coding classes for?

They are designed for children aged 8–11 who already read fluently. A 7-year-old can join with a parent beside them.

Do we need to install anything or have a special laptop?

No installation is needed. The class runs in the browser on an ordinary laptop or computer.

How is it different from Scratch?

The look and block names follow Scratch 3, plus ordered lessons, robot practice, quizzes and automatic checking. Projects open in Scratch, and the other way round.

What can we try for free?

The first two lessons, with no credit card and no install.

Is it safe for children?

There are no ads and no public chat. Comments exist only in the class gallery, are short, and can be removed by the teacher.

Do children get a certificate?

Yes, there is a graduation certificate after finishing the curriculum.

In what language are the lessons?

The lessons are in Indonesian.
